Pigmentation in Indian skin: why gentle beats aggressive

Dark spots, melasma, sun spots and the marks acne leaves behind are among the most common reasons people visit an aesthetic clinic in India. Melanin-rich skin protects well against sun damage — but it also reacts to inflammation by making more pigment. That single fact shapes how pigmentation should be treated.

Why "stronger" can backfire

Heat and irritation can trigger post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation — new darkening after a treatment. In studies on melasma, higher-energy laser settings have been linked to this rebound in some patients, which is why many dermatologists now favour low-energy "laser toning" over multiple sessions.

What the research points to

  • Low-fluence Q-switched Nd:YAG (1064 nm) is the most widely studied laser for melasma, partly because of its track record in pigmented skin.
  • Spacing matters. One Indian clinical group found that sessions every two weeks worked better than weekly ones, which raised the risk of patchy light spots.
  • Combinations help. A 60-patient Indian trial found that pairing low-fluence laser with a topical (20% azelaic acid) did better than either on its own.
  • Not every laser suits. An Indian expert review advised against older ruby lasers for melasma because of rebound pigmentation and recurrence.
THE TAKEAWAYPigmentation is managed, not "erased in one go". A good plan combines gentle in-clinic treatment, the right home care and daily sunscreen — and is adjusted as your skin responds.

Your part at home

Broad-spectrum sunscreen every morning (and reapplied when outdoors), even in winter and on cloudy days. Avoid picking at spots, harsh scrubs and unprescribed creams — some "fairness" creams contain steroids that can worsen skin long-term.

PRP or GFC? What the studies actually show

Both treatments use your own blood to deliver growth factors to the scalp, aiming to strengthen thinning hair in pattern hair loss.

The difference, simply

  • PRP (platelet-rich plasma) concentrates platelets from your blood, which release growth factors once injected.
  • GFC (growth factor concentrate) processes the blood further so the growth factors are released before injection — a more concentrated, purer preparation.

What the evidence says

Head-to-head research is still small. A 2025 study of 42 patients published in Dermatologic Surgery found GFC improved hair count, shaft thickness and appearance scores more than PRP, with changes visible earlier — while suggesting PRP's benefit may last longer, and that the two could be used in sequence. Larger controlled trials are still needed, so be wary of anyone promising guaranteed regrowth.

THE TAKEAWAYBoth can help the right patient. The first step is finding why hair is falling — thyroid, iron, stress, hormones or genetics — because no injection fixes an untreated underlying cause.

Who tends to do well

People in the earlier stages of thinning, with follicles that are still active, and who are willing to commit to a course of sessions plus maintenance.

The bridal skin timeline, explained

Wedding season in Gujarat runs through the winter months, and clinics see demand climb sharply in the two months before the big dates. The brides and grooms with the best skin on the day almost always started much earlier than that.

6 months out — the slow work

Book a consultation. Start anything that needs a course of sessions: pigmentation and acne-mark treatment, acne-scar work and laser hair reduction.

3 months out — build

Peels, microneedling and hair treatments like PRP or GFC — timed so any redness or flaking is long gone before the functions begin.

4–6 weeks out — refine

Hydration and glow-focused treatments. Lock in your home routine; this is not the time to try new actives.

Final two weeks — keep it calm

Only gentle, familiar treatments, such as a HydraFacial-style session about a week before. Nothing that could leave redness on camera.

DON'T FORGETHD photography and videography pick up more than the face: neck, back, arms and hands. Dermatologists regularly point out back pigmentation, chest acne and dry hands as the areas most often missed.

Why Ahmedabad winters leave your skin tight

From November, Ahmedabad's air turns noticeably drier while the sun stays strong. Skin loses water faster through its outer barrier, which shows up as tightness, flaking, dull patches and sometimes new sensitivity — even in people with oily skin.

Small changes that make a big difference

  • Swap to a gentle cleanser. Foaming, squeaky-clean washes strip what little moisture is left.
  • Moisturise on damp skin, morning and night. Look for ceramides, glycerin or hyaluronic acid.
  • Keep sunscreen daily. Winter sun still drives pigmentation; this is when many people's dark spots quietly return.
  • Go easy on actives. If retinoids or acids start to sting, reduce how often you use them rather than pushing through.
  • Short, warm — not hot — showers, and don't forget lips and hands.
IN CLINICWinter is a good season for hydration-focused facials, and for starting pigmentation and laser courses — with less sweat and sun than summer, aftercare is easier to follow.

Laser hair reduction on Indian skin: 5 things to know

1. It's reduction, not instant removal

Laser targets hair in its active growth phase, so a course of sessions spaced a few weeks apart is normal. Most people see a substantial, long-lasting reduction, with occasional maintenance.

2. The device and settings must suit your skin

Darker skin also contains melanin that can absorb laser energy, so wavelength and settings are chosen to reach the hair while protecting the skin around it. This is a medical decision, not a salon one.

3. Shave, don't wax, before sessions

The laser needs the hair root in place. Waxing, threading and plucking remove it; shaving doesn't.

4. Sun before and after is the main risk

A recent tan increases the chance of irritation or pigment changes. Protect the area and tell us about any recent sun exposure.

5. Hormones can matter

For unwanted facial hair in women, conditions like PCOS can keep driving growth. We may suggest checking that alongside treatment for better, longer-lasting results.

THE TAKEAWAYDone properly, laser hair reduction is one of the most satisfying treatments we offer, for men and women. Done carelessly, it's the one most likely to cause burns or pigmentation. Choose the doctor, not the deal.
